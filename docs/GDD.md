## 1. Vision

You are the mind of an ant colony. You start with a queen and a handful of workers in a small hole under the grass. You dig down through increasingly strange layers of earth, discover ores and secrets, and turn your colony into a living machine: ants and silk conveyors carry resources through tunnels to processing chambers and to the **Queen**, who turns them into eggs, new castes, and evolutions.

The fun comes from three layers stacked on top of each other:

- ✅ **Active play** — you personally dig, click, use abilities, react to events, and fight threats. It feels juicy and responsive. *(Click-to-dig with combo and debris, four abilities, four random events, raids and cave creatures, and ant possession: take control of any ant and dig, carry and fight with it yourself.)*
- 🟡 **Automation** — you design tunnels, conveyors, lifts and production chains so the colony runs itself. Watching resources flow to the queen should be satisfying, like Factorio in an ant farm. *(Ants mine, haul and build; specialist castes; silk and iron belts and lifts, splitters, filters, chests, unloaders, storage filters, a crusher, a smelter and a fungus garden; pheromone trails and priorities kept alive by emitters. Fluid- and heat-powered machines not yet.)*
- ✅ **Incremental progression** — numbers still go up, but through *unlocks, new mechanics, new biomes and prestige*, not just "+10% mining speed". *(Research tree with unlocks and stat upgrades, contracts, offline progress, seven biomes, and the Nuptial Flight prestige with a Genetic Memory tree and world modifiers.)*

Design pillars:
1. ✅ **Always something to do** — a new goal is always visible on screen. *(The contract board.)*
2. 🟡 **Watchable** — the colony is alive; hundreds of ants climbing walls, carrying things, reacting. *(Climbing, carrying, foraging, eggs done; reactions and more behaviours to come.)*
3. ✅ **Depth = discovery** — every layer down introduces new materials, mechanics and dangers. *(Seven biomes: water, sand, gas, lava, ruins and a creature per deep layer.)*
4. 🟡 **Automate what you've mastered** — anything you did by hand early becomes automatable later. *(Belts and machines take over hauling and processing, emitters keep scents alive; genetic traits make early research inherited and let ants follow ore veins on their own (Vein Instinct). More later.)*

## 2. Game design

### 2.1 Core loop
✅ Dig → discover resources → haul them (ants, then conveyors) → process them in chambers → feed the Queen and build/research → get more ants, castes and tech → dig deeper → hit a wall → **prestige** (Nuptial Flight) → start a new colony stronger and with new options.
*(The whole loop exists: princesses raised in a Princess Chamber fly off, the Genetic Memory they earn buys permanent traits, and the next colony starts on a new world with the modifiers the player picked. See 2.12.1.)*

### 2.2 The world (side cross-section)
- ✅ Procedurally generated from a seed, wide horizontally (1024 cells), deep vertically (1536 cells), with a **surface** at the top (grass, plants and sky).
  - ✅ Day/night and weather (see 2.8.1).
- ✅ **Depth layers / biomes**, each with its own materials, hazards, enemies and visual palette (details in 2.9.1):
  1. ✅ Topsoil & roots — dirt, pebbles, grubs, copper, sand, roots under the surface plants
  2. ✅ Clay & groundwater — clay, iron, sand, water pockets that flood tunnels
  3. ✅ Stone strata — hard rock, coal, fossils, amber, centipedes *(amber's ancient bugs as research/collectibles not yet)*
  4. ✅ Crystal caverns — big caves full of light-emitting crystals, lakes, cave spiders; crystal resonance (2.2.2)
  5. ✅ Fungal depths — glowing giant fungi (food), spore gas pockets, swarms of fungal mites (the hostile underground species)
  6. ✅ Magma zone — basalt, obsidian, lava lakes, gas pockets, fire beetles; magma rock and lava heat smelters (2.2.1)
  7. ✅ Ancient ruins — rooms of a lost ant civilization, relics (knowledge), tablets, ruin guardians; their story, the ancient research tier and the Ancient Engine (2.2.2)
- ✅ Materials have hardness, value, and special properties (light-emitting, flammable, conductive, unstable, hot). *(Hardness, value, light emission, unstable (sand, ash collapse), flammable (fire, 2.2.1), hot (magma rock heats smelters) and conductive (copper and iron ore carry crystal resonance, glow crystals resonate; 2.2.2).)*
- ✅ **Cave features**: natural caverns ✅, underground lakes ✅, gas pockets ✅, lava lakes ✅, collapsing sand ✅, ruin rooms ✅, roots ✅ (2.8.1).
- ✅ **Fluids**: cellular water, lava and gas that flow into tunnels. Flooding is a hazard ✅, and a resource ✅: pumps, water wheels, irrigation for fungus farms and aphid ranches, magma heat (2.2.1).
- ✅ **Fire**: coal, roots, mycelium and giant fungus burn; fire spreads, smokes and burns into ash (2.2.1).
- ✅ **Fog of war / darkness**: unexplored areas are dark; breaking into a hidden cave reveals it. Light matters (see 3.2).

### 2.2.1 The living world: fluids and materials (as built in Milestone 10.3) ✅
*(Decided: no fluid pressure (pumps give the player control over water; lakes and floods stay predictable) and no power network (water wheels and magma heat boost machines next to them).)*
- **Fluid blocks**: finished pumps and water wheels mark their cells `CellFlags.FluidBlock`; the fluid automaton treats them as closed (ants still walk through). The flag is cleared on load and set again by the buildings.
- **Pump** (1 cell, 4-way arrow, R turns it, `BuildingKind.Pump`; 3 copper + 2 root fibre, research *Hydraulics*): every fluid step it moves water, gas or smoke (never lava) from behind it to where its spout points, `speed` (2) cells of fluid per second. Its intake reaches up to `pumpReach` (8) cells back through open cells to the first one holding fluid; its outlet pushes on along the arrow past cells already full of that fluid (up to 8) to the first with room. Without pressure this is what lets a pump above a flooded shaft drain it and lift the water up. It stops when the outlet holds another fluid (*output blocked*) or nothing is behind it (*idle*).
- **Water Wheel** (2 × 2, no floor, `BuildingKind.WaterWheel`; 4 copper, 2 iron, 4 root fibre, research *Water Mills*): water resting on top of it falls through it each fluid step, up to `speed` (2) cells per second per column, into the cells below if they have room. Its **power** (0..1, smoothed over about a second) is the share of its full rate that passed. Machines within `boostRadius` (8) cells (gap between footprints) work `machineBoost` (+50%) × power faster; the best wheel counts, wheels don't stack. A pump lifting the water back is a valid loop (its cost is the price; there is no power grid). Water spreads slowly along long flat channels, so wheels belong under waterfalls or pump outlets.
- **Irrigation** (`BuildingDef.irrigationBonus`, 0.5 on the Fungus Garden and Aphid Ranch): when a craft finishes and an open cell in or right around the machine holds water ≥ `irrigationMinLevel` (24/255), the machine drinks `irrigationWater` (48/255) from the wettest one and adds the bonus to a carry; every whole point is an extra batch of the recipe's outputs (so +50% = every second craft doubled; deterministic).
- **Magma heat** (`BuildingDef.heatBonus`, 1.0 on the Smelter): a machine with lava, a `Hot` material (magma rock) or a fire in or right around its footprint is **heated** (checked every 0.5 s): it works +100% faster and skips **fuel** inputs (`ItemDef.fuel`: coal) — it stops taking them in (its active mask drops them, so belts, workers and routing follow) and its recipes start without them.
- **Fire** (`FireSystem`, `FluidKind.Fire` on a *solid* cell, level = fire steps left; saved, uploaded and drawn with the fluids, skipped by the automaton): a solid, flammable, revealed cell that touches an open cell without water (a fire needs air; lava and gas don't smother it) can burn. **Moving lava** lights flammable neighbours (chance `lavaIgniteChance` 5% per lava step, proposed by the fluid job), and a **meteorite** lights everything that can burn within its crater + 3 cells (roots under plants). Each fluid step a burning cell lights each flammable neighbour with chance `spreadPerSecond` (0.25/s), gives off `smokePerStep` (4/255) of **smoke** into the open cell above (else beside, else below), and burns down; after `burnSeconds` (15 s) it becomes **ash** (a soft, unstable material that falls like sand when undermined and is then ordered for mining; flags such as mining orders stay). Water ≥ `extinguishLevel` (32/255) next to it puts it out (losing 16/255 as steam); digging the cell out ends it. Ants next to a burning cell take `fireDamage` (10/s, *Burned*; hazard resistance applies). Burning cells emit light. At most `maxBurning` (2000) at once. Fire only starts while the game runs (no fire offline).
- **Smoke** (`FluidKind.Smoke`): behaves like gas (rises, spreads under ceilings, hurts ants from 19% density, gas-proof creatures ignore it) but is grey and thins out everywhere, not only in explored tunnels.
- **Materials**: coal, roots, mycelium and giant fungus are flammable; magma rock is hot; copper ore, iron ore and glow crystals are conductive (crystal resonance, 2.2.2).
- **Presentation**: the terrain shader draws burning cells (flickering flames on open edges, a charred face with sparkling embers inside, flames licking up from burning floors) and smoke as a grey haze; pump (impeller turning while it pumps, spout = outlet) and water wheel (four frames, turning as fast as its power) sprites; the flow overlay tints pumps and wheels by status; hover texts for pumps, wheels, burning cells, smoke and machines' heat / irrigation / wheel boost; a red **Fire!** card (cells burning, Go); a crackle when a fire starts and the lava hiss when water puts one out.
- **Content**: Pump, Water Wheel, research *Hydraulics* and *Water Mills* (row 9), material Ash, contracts *Waterworks* (build pumps) and *Mill Race* (build a water wheel). Save: `FLUD` 2 (see 3.5).

### 2.2.2 The deep endgame: crystals and ruins (as built in Milestone 10.5) ✅
*(Decided while building: the Ancient Engine is placed by the player, but only in the ruins biome, rather than generated in the world; it is restored in paid steps that workers build like blueprints; lore is found by studying (no separate "decipher" action); rival ants are a raid variant, the mole cricket a new timed threat.)*
- **Crystal resonance** (`BuildingKind.Resonator`, `BuildingSystem.Ruins.cs`): the **Crystal Resonator** (2 × 2 on a floor; 8 crystal shards, 4 copper, 2 iron; research *Crystal Resonance*, tier 6) must touch a solid **conductive** cell (`MaterialFlags.Conductive`: glow crystals, copper and iron ore) in or around its footprint (`PlaceResult.NeedsCrystal`). Every climate check (0.5 s) it follows the conductive cells it touches (4-way, at most `maxClusterCells` 400) and sums their `TerrainMaterialDef.resonance` (glow crystal 1; ores 0, they only carry it). **Power** = sum ÷ `fullResonance` (24), at most 1, kept in `Building.Craft`. It makes `knowledgePerSecond` (0.25 → 15 a minute) × power (knowledge gain applies; studied knowledge, so it counts in offline progress) and machines within `boostRadius` (8) work `machineBoost` (+30%) × power faster (`Machine.ResonanceBoost`; the best resonator counts; it stacks with a water wheel). Mining the cluster weakens it.
- **Lore** (`LoreDef`, `LoreSystem`): 12 fragments of the story of the lost colony (the *Old Colony*: crystal song, square halls, guardians, the Engine, the fire that silenced it, the last flight, a message to its heirs). A fragment is found once `count` of its `item` has been studied in this colony (`ColonyStats.Studied`, counted per input of every knowledge recipe): nine from **relics** (1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18), three from **ancient tablets** (1, 2, 3). Found fragments stay found (saved by id).
- **Tablets**: a material (`tablet`, glowing gold, hardness 6) set at world generation into the floors of ruin rooms (`WorldGenSettings.tabletMaterial`, `tabletCount` 4, at least `tabletSpacing` 60 cells apart; the solid cell under an open room cell with solid floor on both sides); it drops the **Ancient Tablet** item; the Archive studies one in 20 s for 60 knowledge.
- **The ancient tier** (research tier 7, `ResearchDef.loreRequired`, `ResearchResult.NeedsLore`): *Ancient Masonry* (3 fragments; build speed +30%, defense +25%), *Resonant Lattice* (5; machine speed +25%), *The Ancient Engine* (8; unlocks it).
- **The Ancient Engine** (`BuildingKind.Engine`, 4 × 3, 4 relics + 10 copper): `unique` (`PlaceResult.Unique`), only in `requiredBiome` (the ruins; `PlaceResult.WrongBiome`, checked with the world's biome scale). Once cleared (built) it stands at stage 0; `BuildingDef.stages` are its restoration steps (*Iron Gears* 30 iron + 10 copper, 40 s; *Crystal Heart* 30 crystal shards + 6 amber, 40 s; *The Waking* 8 relics + 12 obsidian + 150 food, 60 s). `EnqueueStage` pays the next one and turns the engine into a blueprint (`Building.Restoring`) for workers; finishing it raises `Building.Stage` (the sprite frame). Removing it during a step cancels the step (refunded); a standing engine can't be removed (`PlaceResult.Permanent`). After the last step it **runs**: `knowledgePerSecond` 1 (60 a minute) and the flight's Genetic Memory × (1 + `PrestigeConfig.engineMemoryBonus`, 1) = × 2 (`FlightEstimate.EngineFactor`).
- **Rival ants** (`EnemyBehavior.Thief`, the *Rival Ant*: 28 HP, bite 5): from `rivalFirstSeconds` (25 minutes) a raid is rival ants with `rivalRaidChance` (40%) instead of termites (same size, warning and spawn). In the royal chamber each grabs the most stocked non-food item every `stealSeconds` (1.2 s), up to `carryItems` (3), then leaves; it carries the loot drawn in its jaws. A killed thief drops its loot as a pile; items carried off are lost. A raid that lost an item is not repelled; repelled rival raids are counted (`RivalsRepelled`).
- **Mole cricket** (`EnemyBehavior.Digger`, 140 HP, bite 12 / 1.2 s, `digPower` 1, `maxDigHardness` 2): from `moleFirstSeconds` (18 minutes, with `moleMinAnts` 15) every 8–14 minutes one starts in soft ground `moleDistance` (26–42) cells from the queen and at least `moleClearance` (8) from explored tunnels, in state `Burrow`. Each decision it steps (or digs) to the 4-neighbour closest to the queen, with a little random wobble, never straight back, through material no harder than its limit (dirt, pebbles, clay, sand, ash, packed earth: earth walls don't stop it; stone, roots and ore do). Dug cells open without drops and are revealed with their neighbours, so its tunnel shows through the fog. Next to explored open ground it **breaks in**: its tunnel becomes explored (no cave event) and it hunts like a cave creature around the nest. Without getting closer for `moleGiveUpSeconds` (30) it gives up (removed).
- **Presentation**: resonator (idle / resonating) and engine (one frame per stage) sprites, the rival ant and the mole cricket (walk and dig frames), the tablet tile; the **Codex** panel (L, toolbar button once a fragment is found); the engine's steps in the configure panel (I or a click); research tier 7 marked *ancient* with lore requirements; cards for rival raids and the mole cricket; toasts for lore, engine steps and refusals; hover texts; sound cues (lore, engine step and waking, burrowing, stolen items) and the music cue slots `music_lore` and `music_engine`.
- **Content**: item *Ancient Tablet*, material *tablet*, recipe *study a tablet*, buildings *Crystal Resonator* and *Ancient Engine*, 4 research nodes, 12 lore fragments, enemies *Rival Ant* and *Mole Cricket*, contract *Singing Stone* (build resonators), achievements *Crystal Choir*, *Keeper of the Codex*, *The Engine Wakes* (hidden), *Not One Crumb*. Save: `BLDG` 4, `THRT` 2, `RUIN` 1 (3.5).

### 2.3 Ants and castes
- ✅ Ants are individual agents that **climb walls and ceilings** — they crawl on any surface of a dug tunnel, including the back wall (like an ant farm), so they take direct routes through chambers. Above ground they must touch the ground.
- ✅ Castes unlocked over time: **Worker** (dig/haul) ✅, **Miner** (fast digging) ✅, **Hauler** (carries more) ✅, **Nurse** (brood care) ✅, **Builder** (constructs chambers/conveyors) ✅, **Soldier** (defense) ✅, **Scout** (explores, reveals map, finds events) ✅, **Engineer** (maintains machines) ✅, **Glow Ant** (carries light) ✅ and **Fire Ant** (heat-proof, for the magma zone) ✅. *(See 2.6.1 and 2.3.1.)*
- ✅ Ants gain **XP/levels** in their job and may hatch with a small trait ("Strong", "Swift", "Lucky", "Lazy"). *(10.4, see 2.3.1. Per-ant needs (hunger, rest) were dropped (decision 10, 2026-11-09): food is a colony-wide upkeep per caste.)*
- ✅ **Job system** with priorities. The player gives orders mainly through **designations** (dig here, build this) and **pheromone painting** (see 2.6), not by micromanaging individuals. *(Mining orders, storage zones, blueprints, dig/haul/build jobs with weights and openings; Dig / Gather scents put jobs first, Avoid keeps ants out; job weights per caste; a priority 1–5 per job in the priority panel since 10.4.)*
### 2.3.1 Colony depth (as built in Milestone 10.4) ✅
*(Decided: evolutions are paid with rare finds from the stock, no new currency; the queen has no hit points, so the health / regeneration evolutions work on her brood.)*
- **Glow Ant** (`AntCaste.Glow`, research *Glow Ants*, after Scout Caste): a worker (digs and builds at 0.8) whose gaster glows (`CasteDef.lightRadius` 6). It draws an unlit halo; near the camera it carries a real light (3.2).
- **Fire Ant** (`AntCaste.Fire`, research *Fire Ants*, after Engineer Caste): a sturdy worker (60 HP, digs 1.2×), `heatProof` (no fire or heat damage) and `lavaDamageTaken` 0.25.
- **Heat** (hazard): an ant next to lava, a `Hot` material (magma rock) or a burning cell takes `CombatConfig.heatDamage` (3/s); every biome has its own `heat` (the magma zone 0.8/s, mapped to rows of the world); hazard resistance divides it; death cause *Heat*.
- **Queen evolutions** (`EvolutionDef`: icon, tree position, one cost per level, prerequisites, effects; `EvolutionSystem`): per colony, lost at a flight. Effects: a stat per level, or a caste / ability unlocked from a given level. Six in the content: *Bigger Broods* (laying +15%/level, honeydew), *Royal Cradles* (+2 egg slots/level), *Hardy Brood* (+15% ant hit points), *Quick Mending* (+50% healing, stat `AntRegen`), *Deep Lineage* (glow ants at level 1, fire ants at level 2, without research) and *Quickening* (the ability: every egg hatches at once, 5 minute recharge). Each column needs a level of the one to its left. Costs mix honeydew, amber, fossils, chitin, crystal shards, obsidian, star fragments, relics and food, so rare finds now compete between the Archive and the queen.
- **Queen panel** (H, click the queen): laying interval, eggs, upkeep, skilled ants and traits, a shortcut to the caste mix, the evolution tree.
- **Job priorities** (priority panel, P): per job (dig, haul, build, supply, tend, nurse) 1–5, multiplying the job's distance by ×4 / ×2 / ×1 / ×0.5 / ×0.25 (`AntGrowthConfig`); caste job weights stay. Nurses and engineers weigh their own job (`roleWeight` 0.1 × its priority) against the shared jobs they may take; since 10.4 nurses may haul and engineers supply machines (weight 2), so they help while their own job waits.
- **Experience**: an ant's first job becomes its **skill**; only work in it gives XP (dig / build per unit of work, haul / supply per item, tend per second at a working machine, nurse per egg laid in a nursery). Levels 1–5 (15 / 45 / 110 / 240 XP) give +5% per level at that job (dig and build power, walking while hauling / supplying / nursing, an engineer's machine boost) and a slight preference for it.
- **Traits** at hatch (30%, equally likely): *Strong* carries one more, *Swift* walks ×1.2, *Lucky* gets an extra drop from 25% of the cells it breaks, *Lazy* works and walks ×0.8.
- **Lane jitter**: every ant has a lane (−127..127): ±7% walking speed, and drawn up to 3 art pixels ahead of / behind its spot and 3 above / 2 below the surface it walks on, so a column on one route reads as a stream.
- **Save**: `ANTS` 4 and `QUEN` 1 (3.5).

- ✅ The player can **possess** any ant (click to take control) for direct active play: run, dig, carry, fight. This is the main "active" mode and should feel good to control. *(See 2.6.1; it uses the same crawling rules as every ant.)*

### 2.4 The Queen
- ✅ The heart of the colony and the main resource sink. Lives in the Royal Chamber (the starting nest).
- ✅ Consumes food and special resources → lays eggs → eggs need nurses and brood chambers to hatch. *(Food → eggs of the caste mix the player sets → ants. Nurses carry eggs to Nurseries where they hatch faster; nurses are a boost, not a requirement (decision M6). **Honeydew** (2.8.1): the queen eats it from the stock and lays faster. Rare finds pay for her evolutions.)*
- ✅ **Queen Evolution tree**: spend rare resources to unlock castes, bigger broods, passive bonuses, new abilities. *(10.4, see 2.3.1: per colony; castes come from research or from the *Deep Lineage* evolution.)*
- ✅ Visually central: conveyors and haulers feeding her, eggs being carried away. If she starves, production slows dramatically (clear feedback, not instant game over).

### 2.5 Logistics and automation
- ✅ **Early**: ants carry everything by hand. Visible bottlenecks motivate automation.
- ✅ **Silk conveyors**: belts laid along tunnels (left/right); items visibly ride them.
- ✅ **Root lifts / elevators**: vertical transport (up/down) for deep mining.
- ✅ **Splitters, filters, mergers, buffers** for routing. *(Splitters, filters, merging, chests as buffers and unloaders that put stock back onto belts: see 2.5.3.)*
- ✅ **Storage chambers** with capacity: items are kept physically in storage cells (8 each, any type, sorted by placement) and in the royal chamber, and drawn there.
- ✅ **Pheromone highways**: painted trails that make ants travel faster and prefer routes.
- 🟡 Throughput stats and overlays (show flow rates, blocked belts, idle ants) so optimizing feels like a puzzle. *(Flow overlay, income rates per item and a hover panel with per-belt flow done; numbers drawn in the world not yet.)*

### 2.5.1 Colony basics (as built in Milestone 2) ✅
- **Resources**: digging a cell may drop an item (`TerrainMaterialDef.drop`): ores, coal, amber, fossils, crystal shards, grubs. Plain dirt/clay/stone drop nothing. Drops fall straight down to the floor as piles.
- **Food**: grub nests in the topsoil (dig them → grubs) and seeds that appear on the surface over time (`ContentDatabase.surfaceForage`), so workers go outside to forage. Food items turn into Food points on delivery.
- **Delivery**: workers haul items to the royal chamber (around the queen) or to **Storage zones** the player paints on open cells. *(Changed in M3: the stock is physical. The royal chamber holds `baseItemCapacity` (40) items and every storage cell `storageItemsPerCell` (8), any mix of types. A delivered item goes into the nearest container with room, preferring one that already holds that item, and every stored item is drawn on the back wall of its cell / the royal chamber floor. Erasing a storage cell moves its items to other containers or drops them. Food stays as points with its own capacity.)* Ants only pick up what can still be delivered.
- **Orders**: the player paints **Mine** orders on solid cells (also into the fog, to plan tunnels ahead) and Storage zones; right mouse erases. Personal click-dig stays as the **Dig** tool.
- **Queen**: consumes food to lay eggs on the chamber floor; eggs hatch into workers. Workers and queen have food upkeep. At zero food the colony is **starving**: work slows and laying stops (no game over).
- **Jobs**: idle workers choose dig or haul by path distance and priority weights, limited by open slots (a few diggers per frontier cell, one hauler per item) so crowds don't queue behind a narrow tunnel front.
- **Watchability**: colony sprites never render darker than a minimum brightness, so the colony stays readable in dark tunnels while terrain darkness still shows where light is.

### 2.5.2 Logistics (as built in Milestone 3) ✅
- **Building**: the Build tool places **blueprints**. The cost is paid from the stock when placed; workers walk over and build it (a build job next to dig and haul). Removing a building refunds its cost (blueprints always in full; finished buildings by `refundFraction`, default 100%).
- **Transport cells** (1 cell, on open walkable cells, underground or on the ground surface): **Silk Belt** (left/right), **Root Lift** (up/down), **Splitter**, **Filter**. A cell holds up to 2 items. Items move along the cell and pass to the next transport cell; when the next one is full they wait, so jams back up the line. Belts feeding into one cell merge. Two belts pointing at each other never connect.
- **End of a line**: a belt or lift whose next cell has no transport delivers into a **machine** there, or into the **stock** if the line ends in (or next to) the royal chamber or a storage zone. Otherwise the line leads nowhere and items stop at its end.
- **Splitter**: hands items in turn to every neighbouring belt, lift or machine that is not feeding into it. **Filter**: items of its chosen type leave only in its arrow direction; everything else goes to its other sides. Splitters and filters do not connect directly to each other.
- **Routing masks**: every transport cell knows which items can reach a sink from it. A splitter only sends an item where it can be delivered; workers only load belts that can deliver what they carry; falling drops only land on such belts.
- **Workers and belts**: a carrying worker picks once between the stock (royal chamber, storage zones and belts leading there) and a machine that needs the item (or a belt feeding one), preferring machines unless they are much farther away (`consumerBias`). Workers drop items onto the nearest suitable belt instead of walking all the way. Drops from digging fall onto a belt or lift they pass. A worker with nowhere to deliver puts its load down as a pile.
- **Machines** (multi-cell, on a flat floor): input buffer per item (two crafts' worth); they pick the first craftable recipe in turn. Products go onto a transport cell next to the machine that leads away from it, or are left as a pile on the machine floor for workers (production pauses at `outputLimit`). Machines are fed by belts, by workers bringing fresh piles, and by workers **fetching from storage**: a supply job (per consumed item, a flow field to the containers holding it) takes an item out of the container the worker reaches and carries it to the machine, as long as machines have room for more than is already on its way.
- **Content**: Smelter (3×3): 2 copper ore → 1 copper; 2 iron ore + 1 coal → 1 iron. Fungus Garden (4×2): 2 seeds → 3 fungus (3 food each), the first food multiplier. Copper is used for filters; iron has no use yet.
- **Overlay (O)**: belts coloured from grey (idle) to green (flowing), red when jammed; machines green (working), yellow (waiting for input), red (output blocked); idle workers get a blue marker. The resource bar shows income per minute (net for food).

### 2.5.3 Logistics completion (as built in Milestone 10.1) ✅
- **Chest** (1 cell, 4 copper ore, no research): a container of 16 items (`BuildingDef.capacity`) that counts as storage. A belt or lift whose line ends at a chest puts items straight into it (if its filter allows; otherwise the nearest storage with room). A worker standing on a chest delivers into it first. Chests can't go on a storage zone and storage can't be painted over a chest. Removing one moves its items to other storage (what doesn't fit drops). It shows goods on its lid with a dot in the colour of what it holds most of.
- **Storage filters**: every container (storage cell, chest; the royal chamber always takes everything) has a set of allowed items. **I** on a storage cell sets the filter of the whole zone (every storage cell connected to it); on a chest, that chest. Deliveries skip containers that refuse the item; an item nothing takes is no longer worth hauling (acceptance counts free room in the containers that take it). Items already inside stay.
- **Reserve (keep for building)**: per item, a number the colony keeps in stock. Supply jobs (workers fetching for machines) and unloaders never take it below that; building costs still can. Set in the stock view (click an item in the resource bar).
- **Unloader** (1 cell, 3 copper, research *Stockkeeping*): takes one item at a time (its item, or any) out of the container behind it (storage cell, chest or royal chamber cell, opposite its arrow) and hands it to the belt, lift, router or machine its arrow points at, at `speed` (1.5) items per second times belt speed, only when that output can take the item now, never below the reserve. **I** picks the item; R turns new ones.
- **Recipe choice**: a machine with several recipes runs any it can (round-robin, as before) or one chosen recipe; then it only takes in that recipe's inputs (room for the others is 0, so belts, workers and routing masks follow). What is already buffered stays.
- **Crusher** (2×2, 10 copper ore + 4 copper, research *Ore Crushing*): 2 copper ore → 3 crushed copper (3 s), 2 iron ore → 3 crushed iron (4 s). The smelter smelts 2 crushed copper → 1 copper (3 s) and 2 crushed iron + 1 coal → 1 iron (5 s): 75% more metal per ore, the first three-step chain.
- **Tiers**: Iron Belt (1 iron, 5 cells/s) and Iron Lift (1 iron + 1 copper ore, 6 cells/s), research *Reinforced Silk*; Iron Gate (3 iron + 2 copper, 450 HP), research *Iron Bulwark*. `BuildingDef.tier` picks the sprite. **Placing a building of the same kind over another tier upgrades it in place**: the old one is refunded, the new one paid, and the cell becomes a blueprint for workers (items riding a belt stay). Chests never swap.
- **Routers next to routers**: splitters and filters now connect directly. An item remembers the building it came from (`ItemSlot.From`) and a router never hands it back. Routing masks are kept per router and incoming side (`5` per router: the four neighbours and "from nowhere"), so a router only counts what its neighbour router can deliver without going back through it; a line feeding a router uses the "from nowhere" mask.
- **Configure panel** (I, rebindable): storage zone / chest filters (item toggles, All, None), unloader item (or any), filter item, emitter scent, machine recipe. Clicking a resource entry opens the stock view (reserves). The hover panel tells what each takes and which key configures it.
- **Research** (row 7): Stockkeeping (tier 2), Ore Crushing (3), Reinforced Silk (4, also needs Silk Weaving), Iron Bulwark (5, also needs Fortification). **Contracts**: build chests, make crushed copper, lay iron belts. **Achievements**: *Quartermaster* (10 chests), *Iron Highway* (100 iron belts).

### 2.6 Pheromones (signature mechanic) ✅
The player paints pheromone fields onto the map: *Trail*, *Dig*, *Gather*, *Defend*, *Avoid*. Ants follow gradients. This is the main way to direct the colony and should be thematic and fun. Pheromones fade over time unless sustained by a **Pheromone Emitter** building (automation of orders).
*(Changed in M6: **Trail** (the highways of 2.5) replaces *Haul to*, which storage zones already do. Scents do not diffuse: painted areas stay sharp and predictable, and the flow fields read them as on / off. Details in 2.6.1.)*

### 2.6.1 Pheromones & castes (as built in Milestone 6) ✅
- **Pheromones** (`PheromoneSystem`): one strength byte per kind and cell (255 = fresh). The Pheromone tool (5) paints a round brush on cells the player can see; right mouse erases that kind. Strength drops by `decayPerSecond` (1) once a second, so a painted scent lasts about 4 minutes; a Burst job only visits chunks that hold some. Ants carrying something refresh the trail cell they step on (+6). A **Pheromone Emitter** (research *Scent Glands*) keeps every cell of its kind within 10 cells at full strength (paint first, then the emitter keeps it) and always scents its own spot; R picks the kind of new ones, clicking a placed one changes it.
  - **Trail**: a step between two trail cells costs half in the flow fields (1 / 2 instead of 2 / 3) and ants walk 50% faster on it.
  - **Dig** / **Gather**: mining orders / item piles inside the scent are sources at distance 0; all other ones start "late" at 40 cells (`priorityCells`), so scented jobs win unless a plain one is 40 cells closer. Arrival is detected at local minima, so late sources still work.
  - **Defend**: scented cells join the guard field; idle soldiers patrol there (like guard posts). Only available once soldiers are researched.
  - **Avoid**: a step touching an Avoid cell costs 4×; no dig, haul or build job is taken inside; wandering ants never step in. Fields enemies follow (home, exit, ants, enemies) ignore all pheromones, so scents can't steer raiders.
  - Drawn by the terrain shader as static speckles in the scent's colour (faint normally, clearer with the Pheromone tool), mirrored to the GPU as 2 bits per kind and cell.
- **Castes** are data (`CasteDef`): egg cost, upkeep, hit points, bite, speed / dig / build multipliers, items carried, job weights (0 = never) and specialist numbers. Workers and soldiers took over the numbers that were in `GameConfig`. Specialists are unlocked by research. The player sets a **share** per caste in the castes panel (K, 5% steps, at most 50% each and 80% together; new castes start at 10%, soldiers 20%); the queen lays whichever unlocked caste is furthest below its share (by at least half an ant), else a worker.

- **Scouts** walk to the edge of the known tunnels (a "frontier" field: explored tunnel cells with hidden rock 8 cells away, recomputed by a parallel Burst job at most every 3 s) and twice a second reveal every hidden cell within 10 cells; ore found that way glows like Seismic Sense. Left mouse bites toward the cursor within 2.5 cells: an enemy there takes 6 damage, else the rock is dug like the Dig tool (power 0.9, the combo builds, 6 bites per second). Right mouse or Space picks up a pile at or next to the ant (anything, even if the stock is full), or delivers / puts down what it carries. It walks 60% faster and takes half damage. - **Offline**: castes keep their shares; eggs carried by nurses hatch too; pheromones do not fade while the game is closed.

### 2.8.1 The surface (as built in Milestone 10.2) ✅
*(Decisions, asked and confirmed: a full day and night lasts **12 minutes**; **birds scare first, then kill**.)*
- **Day and night** (`SurfaceState`, `SurfaceConfig`): a clock in days (saved); the time of day is its fraction (0 = midnight). Daylight is 1 from sunrise to sunset (`dayShare` 0.65 of the cycle, so about 7.8 minutes of day and 4.2 of night) with smooth twilights; a colony starts at 0.3 (07:12). **Seeds and leaves only appear by day.** The light map keeps the sky in its own channel, so night needs no relight (3.2): the shaders colour and scale it by the time of day (dim bluish moonlight at night), the sky gradient gets dawn / dusk tints and stars. Dawn and dusk are announced.
- **Weather**: after 10 minutes, every 6–10 minutes, rain (60%) or a drought. **Rain** (60–100 s) drops water each fluid step on random columns within 150 cells of the nest (on the ground, or the bottom of an open shaft); it runs into tunnels and fills dips. Water **within 40 cells of the surface soaks away** (each wet cell loses a level with 30% chance per fluid step), so rain floods drain after a minute or two while natural lakes (deeper) are untouched. **Drought** (3–5 minutes): seeds and leaves half as often. Event cards with the time left. No weather offline.
- **Plants and leaves**: plants stand along the surface at seeded positions (every 8–20 columns, not next to the nest shaft; `SurfacePlants`, derived from the seed, not saved) and live while the ground under them is intact. By day a leaf falls next to a living plant every 6 s (forage rate and weather apply; at most 10 on the surface). Leaves are not food: the **Fungus Garden** takes 2 leaves → 4 fungus (seeds: 2 → 3).
- **Roots** (world generation): from every plant a root strand grows down through the topsoil (10–32 cells, a random walk with side branches, at most 60 deep), replacing plain soil only (not ore or other finds) and stopping at caves. Root is tough (hardness 4, flammable for 10.3) and drops **root fibre** half the time.
- **Aphid Ranch** (4×2, 8 copper ore + 4 root fibre, research *Aphid Herding*: 18 knowledge + 3 root fibre, after Fungiculture): 2 leaves → 1 **honeydew** (8 s); aphids are drawn walking in the pasture. Honeydew stays in the stock (not food points): **the queen eats one every 20 s** while there is any (above the reserve) and meanwhile lays **50% faster** with no upkeep of her own. *(Decision: honeydew stays a stock item so the queen evolution tree (10.4) can spend it.)*
- **Golden aphid** (random event, only with a finished ranch): sits on a ranch floor for 40 s; click it (or a scout catches it) for 10 knowledge, 10 food and 3 honeydew.
- **Birds** (by day, from minute 8, every 2.5–5 minutes, only while an ant is in the open: less than 2 cells below the surface): the bird circles over the ant in the open that is farthest from cover. **Every ant in the open drops its job and runs underground** along a **shelter** flow field (sources: walkable cells 2–3 below the surface, only filled while a bird is around) and waits there. After **8 s** it strikes: ants still in the open within 3 cells of its shadow are taken (at most 4). Sheltering ants are released 4 s later. The possessed ant is left to the player.
- **Wolf spiders** (`EnemyBehavior.NightHunter`, 45 HP, bite 7 / 0.9 s): at nightfall, from minute 15 and with 15 ants, 1 + 0.02 per ant (at most 4, creature modifiers apply) come out on the surface 12–40 cells from the nest over the first minute, lurk until an ant is within 12 cells, then hunt like cave creatures; at dawn they slink off (no loot).
- **Presentation**: swaying plants (dimmed at night), raindrops that stop at the ground, the bird circling / diving with a dithered shadow, a clock and weather entry in the resource bar, cards for weather, the bird (strike countdown) and night hunters, toasts, sound cues (bird cry and strike, rain, dusk, dawn).
- **Content**: items leaf, honeydew, root fibre; material root; recipes *grow fungus on leaves*, *milk the aphids*; Aphid Ranch; research Aphid Herding (row 8); event Golden Aphid; enemy Wolf Spider; contracts *Leaf Litter*, *Root Diggers*, *Sweet Harvest*. Save section `SURF` (see 3.5).

### 2.9.1 Depth & danger (as built in Milestone 5) ✅
- **Biomes** (depth below the surface, borders warped by noise): topsoil 0–110, clay 110–290, stone strata 290–690, **crystal caverns** 690–870 (quartzite, big caves, dense glow crystals, water), **fungal depths** 870–1050 (soft mycelium, glowing giant fungus that drops 2 fungus, spore gas, water), **magma zone** 1050–1250 (basalt, obsidian, glowing magma rock, lava lakes, gas), **ancient ruins** to the bottom (ruin brick in a grid of rooms with doors and hatches instead of noise caves, relics, glow crystals). **Sand** (unstable, soft) appears in the topsoil and clay. Biome extras are data on `BiomeDef`: fluid pockets, a ruin-room grid, and creature lists.
- **Fluids** (`FluidSystem`): water, lava and gas, one kind and a level 0–255 per open cell. A Burst cellular automaton runs 10 times a second over awake chunks only, bottom to top with alternating sweep direction. Water falls, then spreads sideways (half / thirds of the difference); lava does the same every 4th step, only above a difference of 24 and at half the rate, so it heaps up and creeps; gas rises and spreads under ceilings. Water films of 8/255 or less evaporate and gas thins out, but only in explored tunnels (untouched pockets stay). Where lava meets water the lava cell turns into **obsidian** and the water loses 96/255 as steam. Chunks sleep 12 steps after their last change and wake when terrain near them opens; chunks the player has not revealed at all sleep while playing (hidden lakes finish settling when the colony gets close). World generation fills cave regions by noise per biome, drops liquids to the floor / gas to the ceiling per column, then runs up to 400 steps so lakes start settled. *(Decision: a separate 2-byte fluid plane next to the 4-byte cells (both mirrored to the GPU) rather than widening the cell; no pressure, so water does not rise in U-bends.)*
- **Hazards**: water or gas at 50% or more and any lava **block ant paths** (flow fields avoid them; field rebuilds for moving fluid are throttled to once a second). An ant caught inside swims (55% speed) to the dry neighbour nearest home, else up (down out of gas). Damage per second: drowning 6 (deep water), lava 40, gas 4 (from 19% density). **Cave-ins**: when a cell is dug out, unstable cells above it fall one cell per fluid step, sliding diagonally off piles; they never fall into buildings, blueprints, storage or the royal chamber; whoever they land on takes 25 damage and is pushed up; fallen sand is ordered for mining automatically.
- **Health**: workers 40 HP, soldiers 150 HP; ants regenerate 2 HP/s after 6 s without damage. Dead ants drop what they carried.
- **Enemies** (`EnemyDef`, `EnemySystem`): **cave creatures** appear when the colony breaks into a cave (one per N cave cells of that biome, capped per cave, at least 8 cells from the breach), wait dormant until an ant comes within their aggro radius, then hunt along an "ants" flow field and wander near home when they lose their prey. **Raids** (`ThreatConfig`): after 12 minutes and with at least 20 ants, every 7–10 minutes a rival termite raid arrives on the surface 60–100 cells from the nest after a 30 s warning; size 3 + 0.08 per ant + 2 per biome reached below the first, at most 40. Raiders walk to the royal chamber, eat 1 food/s each (the queen stops laying meanwhile) and leave along an exit field once they have eaten 8 or the food is gone. A raid where every raider dies before eating is **repelled** (+10 knowledge). Enemies bite the ant next to them; every ant next to an enemy bites back (workers 3, soldiers 14 damage per second). Killed enemies drop chitin (deep ones also obsidian or relics) and knowledge.

### 2.12.1 Prestige (as built in Milestone 7) ✅
- **Princesses**: research **Nuptial Flight** (tier 6: 80 knowledge, 6 iron; needs Fungiculture and Royal Jelly) unlocks the **Princess Chamber** (4×3, 10 copper + 4 iron). Its recipe *Raise a princess* (12 fungus + 2 amber, 60 s) outputs a princess instead of items (`RecipeDef.princesses`). Princesses are a colony counter (not ants); the chamber pauses while princesses (plus those being raised) reach the limit, `maxPrincesses` (5) + traits. Each chamber shows up to 3 resting princesses.
- **Genetic Memory from a flight** (`PrestigeConfig`): score = deepest dig × 0.1 + knowledge earned × 0.1 + research done × 2 + contracts × 1; memory = floor(√(score / 4) × princess factor × gain), where the princess factor is 1 + 0.25 per princess after the first (counted up to the limit; 0 without princesses) and gain is the `GeneticGain` stat (traits, modifiers). A flight needs at least 1 princess and at least 1 memory. Starting knowledge from traits is not "earned".
- **The flight** (N screen): shows the breakdown, lets the player pick world modifiers (1 slot + traits), and after a confirming second click plays a short flight (princesses fly out of the nest shaft into the sky, 4.5 s, the simulation paused), then founds the next colony on a new random seed with the chosen modifiers and saves at once. `Simulation.TryNuptialFlight` returns the next colony's legacy and leaves the flown colony untouched.
- **Legacy** (`Legacy`): unspent memory, memory earned, flights, every trait's level, best depth and best flight. It is saved inside every colony's save (section `LGCY`), so a flight is atomic: until the new colony is saved, the old save still holds the old colony and the old legacy. *New colony* from the menu keeps the legacy (no memory for the abandoned colony, no modifiers).
- **Genetic traits** (`GeneticDef`, 19 in 4 tiers, levels with growing cost): effects are data — colony **stats** (worker digging / speed, food capacity, laying, max eggs, belt / machine speed, offline efficiency, Genetic Memory gain), **starting** workers / food / knowledge / items and a revealed radius around the nest, **inherited research** (Fungiculture, Silk Routing, Miner and Soldier Caste: done in every colony and at once when bought), extra **modifier slots**, extra **princesses**, **unlocking** world modifiers, and the new mechanic **Vein Instinct** (ore within 2 cells of every dug-out cell is ordered for mining, so ants follow veins). Bought any time: stats and inherited research apply at once, starting bonuses from the next colony.
- **World modifiers** (`WorldModifierDef`, 9): world generation offsets (ore deposit threshold for materials that drop a non-food item, cave threshold, water / lava / gas pocket thresholds, biome thickness multiplier) and colony stats (new stats `RaidSize`, `RaidFrequency`, `CreatureCount`, `GeneticGain`, plus forage). Five start unlocked (Rich Veins, Lush Meadow, Shallow World, Hollow Earth, Quiet Surface); four harsh ones (Flooded Depths, Volcanic, Famine, Swarming) come with the trait *Strange Worlds*. Harder worlds add Genetic Memory gain. Biome lookups at runtime use the world's thickness scale (`Simulation.BiomeAtDepth`).

### 2.12.2 Bloodlines (as built in Milestone 10.6) ✅

- **Respec** (`Simulation.EnqueueRespec`, `RespecPrice`, `Legacy.Spent` / `Reset`): every trait is unlearned and the Genetic Memory spent on it comes back. The first `PrestigeConfig.freeRespecs` (1) respecs of a colony are free; later ones lose `respecFee` (25%) of the refund, rounded up, at least 1. Colony stats stop at once; world modifiers and buildings the traits unlocked lock again (what is built stays); research a trait passed on stays done in this colony only; starting bonuses already given stay. - **New bloodline**: a fresh `Legacy` in the current save slot (Esc menu, two clicks) or in an empty slot (title screen). Achievements belong to the player and stay.
- **Trait-only buildings** (`GeneticEffectKind.UnlockBuilding`): a building a trait names starts locked, is unlocked at once when the trait is bought and in every colony after; it is hidden from the build palette while locked. The **Queen's Throne** (`BuildingKind.Throne`, 4 × 2, 8 amber + 10 copper + 4 iron, unique) is built around the queen (her cell in its bottom row, `PlaceResult.NeedsQueen`). While it stands, `BuildingDef.stats` count (+30% laying, +2 egg slots) and ants within `auraRadius` (12 cells) dig and build `auraWork` (15%) faster (`AntTick.AuraFactor`). The queen is drawn on its dais.
- **Caste lineages**: *Glow Lineage* and *Fire Lineage* (tier 5) make the glow / fire ant research part of every colony, so those castes hatch from the start.
- **Biome variants** (`WorldModifierDef.variants`, `ContentDB.ModifierVariants`, `WorldTweaks.Variants`): a world modifier can give a biome another base material (a copy of the original rock with the same rules and a recoloured tile), another name and other cave creatures. *Wild Lineages* (tier 4, after *Strange Worlds*) unlocks three: **Ember Strata** (ember stone, fire beetles, +20% Genetic Memory), **Drowned Clay** (gley clay, centipedes, +15%), **Amethyst Caverns** (amethyst, fungal mites, +15%).
- **The tree** has a fifth tier (*Glow Lineage*, *Royal Throne*, *Fire Lineage*) and 23 traits in all.

## 3. Technical design

### 3.0 Platform, art direction and scale ✅
- **Engine**: Unity 6.6 (6000.6.x, "Supported" stream), URP 17 with the 2D Renderer. Decision: stay on 6.6 and move to the next LTS when it ships rather than downgrading to 6.3 LTS.
- **Art direction: pixel art.** Everything (terrain, ants, items, buildings, UI accents) is drawn on a single pixel grid. 🟡 *(10.7a: the art moves from sprites generated in code to **PNG files** in `Assets/_Antarium/Art/`, drawn from one palette with flat tones lit from the top left; a PNG replaces the generated sprite of the same slot and the generators stay as the fallback. Rules, palette, sizes and names: `docs/ART_STYLE.md`. Done: all world art (colony with a silhouette per caste, buildings, enemies, surface, every terrain tile), the UI skin and 74 icons.)*
- **Scale**: 16 art pixels per world unit (PPU 16). **1 cell = 8×8 px = 0.5 world units.** An ant is 12×7 px (~1.5 cells long). World 1024 × 1536 cells.
- **Pixel-perfect camera**: zoom levels are integer screen-pixels-per-art-pixel (×1…×8); the camera position snaps to the art-pixel grid. At ×1 on 1080p the view shows ~240 × 135 cells, which is the "overview" zoom. A strategic map beyond that is a separate render, not fractional zoom.

### 3.1 No Tilemaps ✅
The original used Unity Tilemaps and they were a headache (no proper shadows, hard to make look good). **Do not use Tilemaps.** Instead:

- ✅ **World data**: a chunked grid (32×32 cells per chunk, chunk-major so a chunk is one contiguous range) of 4-byte cells: material, density (dig progress), flags (revealed, explored, mining order, storage, blueprint, scanned). This is the single source of truth. *(Fluid lives in a parallel 2-byte plane (level, kind) with the same indexing, uploaded per dirty chunk like the cells.)*
  - A cell is either solid (density eroding from 255 down to 128) or fully dug (0). There is no half-open state.
- ✅ **Terrain rendering: GPU per-pixel "pixel marching squares".** *(Changed from per-chunk procedural meshes because of the pixel-art direction: smooth mesh contours would cut through art pixels.)*
  - Cell data and the light map are mirrored into GPU structured buffers. Only dirty chunks are uploaded, one 1024-cell range each, so digging never re-uploads the whole world.
  - A terrain shader on one world-sized quad snaps every fragment to the art-pixel grid, interpolates the densities of the surrounding cells (the marching-squares field) and thresholds it with smooth value noise. The result is organic, rounded tunnel edges that are still crisp pixel art.
  - Material boundaries are sampled at a smoothly jittered point, so pockets of another material read as blobs, not squares.
  - Surface pixels come from per-material **pixel textures in a texture array** (64×64 since 10.7a, PNG tiles from `Art/Terrain/`), tiled in world pixel space; on top the shader lays **world-space patches** (flat steps of lighter / darker and warmer / cooler ground from value noise), so large areas of one material never show the tile repeating. Edge pixels get outline/rim colors. **Normals are derived from the density gradient** (a bevel) so `Light2D` shades the terrain without hand-drawn normal maps.
  - A darker, low-contrast **background wall** is drawn behind dug areas so tunnels have depth. Its edge against the sky uses the same rounded contour, kept just inside the terrain edge.
  - No per-chunk meshes or GameObjects for terrain.
- ✅ **Colliders: none for terrain.** *(Changed: the possessed ant will use the same grid-based crawling controller as AI ants, so control is consistent and no physics colliders are needed. Revisit only if a feature truly needs Box2D, e.g. physics debris.)*

### 3.2 Lighting and shadows
- ✅ URP **2D Renderer** with `Light2D` (a global light provides the minimum ambient).
- ✅ **Primary darkness comes from a grid light map**: light propagates from the surface (sky light) and from light sources (crystals, giant fungi, magma rock and lava; glow ants use dynamic lights instead, below) using a Burst flood fill (8-way, RGB), sampled by the terrain and sprite shaders. *(Since 10.2 the sky is a separate fourth channel (the former unused alpha): RGB holds emitters, alpha the sky level; the shaders take max(RGB, sky × the sky colour of the time of day and weather), so day and night cost nothing.)* Light is quantized into a few bands per pixel to match the pixel-art look. Updates are exact and local around changes.
- ✅ **ShadowCaster2D only near the camera.** *(Changed from per-chunk everywhere: underground, solid terrain is already dark via the light map, so thousands of casters would cost a lot for almost no visible gain.)* *(10.4, `GlowLights`: around each active glow light, solid cells next to open ones are merged into horizontal runs; each run is a pooled `ShadowCaster2D` whose default unit-square shape is sized by its transform (no private URP API), rebuilt 4 times a second, at most 768. The possessed ant's light still casts no shadows.)*
- 🟡 Dynamic `Light2D`s add local light (possessed ant, glow ants, abilities, explosions) on top of the light map. *(The possessed ant's light; glow ants: the 24 nearest the view centre get a pooled point light, the rest only their halo (10.4). Abilities and explosions not yet.)*
- ✅ Fog of war: unrevealed cells rendered black (a "revealed" bit in the cell flags).
- ✅ Performance budget: lighting must hold 60 FPS with a large, partially dug world *(full recompute once at start, local updates ~1.4 ms)*.

### 3.3 Simulation
- ✅ **Fixed-tick simulation** (20 ticks/sec) fully separated from rendering, which interpolates. This also makes offline progress and saving sane.
- ✅ Ants: target **several hundred to a few thousand** active ants. Store ant state in struct arrays (data-oriented), update with Burst jobs where hot. *(Rendering as built: one dynamic mesh for all colony sprites, written each frame by a Burst job, one draw call. Same cost class as instancing, but it sorts and receives 2D lights like any renderer.)* Sprites snap to the art-pixel grid and rotate in 90° steps to follow the surface. Do not use one GameObject per ant. (The possessed ant can be a normal GameObject.) *(Measured: 1,050 ants ≈ 0.1–0.15 ms per tick.)*
- ✅ **Ant update**: a parallel Burst job moves ants and decides, emitting *intents* for anything touching shared state (dig damage, pickup, delivery); a serial pass applies intents in ant order, so results are deterministic.
- ✅ **Navigation**: ants crawl through dug cells (floor, walls, ceiling and back wall) and, above ground, along the ground. **Flow fields** (distance maps with octile costs: straight 2, diagonal 3; trails 1 / 2, Avoid 4×) per destination kind: mining front, haulable items, drop-off (royal chamber + storage + belts leading there), home, construction sites, guard (posts + Defend scent), brood (eggs for nurses), nurseries with room, frontier (scouts), machines wanting an engineer, golden beetles, the Pheromone Burst spot, shelter from a bird (cells just below the surface), plus per item that a machine uses a "consumer" field (machines needing it, belts feeding them) and a "supply" field (storage holding it). All fields share one array so the Burst ant job can read any of them. Rebuilt in parallel jobs (Dial's algorithm, a ring of 16 buckets) at most every few ticks when something changed. A* for one-off paths is not needed yet.
- ✅ **Pheromones**: grid fields per pheromone type with decay, updated in jobs. *(Changed: one strength byte per kind and cell, no diffusion; flow fields read them as step costs and as "late" sources, see 2.6.1.)*
- ✅ **Fluids**: simple cellular automaton, updated at a lower rate (10 Hz), only in awake (and revealed) chunks; one single-threaded Burst job so results are deterministic. *(See 2.9.1. Since 10.3 cells taken by pumps and water wheels count as closed, burning solid cells and smoke live in the same plane, and the job proposes cells moving lava sets alight; pumps, wheels and fire step on the main thread right after it, see 2.2.1.)*
- ✅ **Enemies**: a plain C# list stepped serially (tens to a few hundred); they read the shared flow fields (home for raiders, an "ants" field while any creature hunts, an exit field while raiders leave), and a per-tick cell → ant hash finds who is next to whom. Soldiers read an "enemies" field (rebuilt while enemies move) and a "guard" field. *(Measured: 1,000 ants 0.09 ms per tick; with 30 cave spiders, 60 soldiers and a 40-termite raid 0.24 ms.)*
- ✅ **Items on conveyors**: simulated as data (up to two item slots per transport cell, not physics objects), stepped downstream-first so lines move without gaps, and rendered by a Burst job into one mesh with the buildings. *(The belt step is plain C# for now: ~0.55 ms per tick for 6,000 loaded belt cells in the editor.)*

### 3.5 Saving ✅
Versioned save format (binary or compressed JSON) including the full world grid, ants, buildings, conveyors, research and prestige. Autosave. Save migration support from day one. Offline progress computed from the save timestamp using a simplified rate-based simulation (not full ant sim).
- *As built:* binary. An uncompressed **header** (magic `ANT2`, format version, save time UTC, seed, play time, ant count, depth: readable without loading) followed by a **deflate-compressed body of tagged sections** (`IDS`, `SIM`, `COLN`, `ANTS`, `BLDG`, `PROG`, `WRLD`, `FLUD`, `THRT`), each with its own version byte. Unknown sections are skipped and missing ones keep defaults; `SaveGame.Migrate` upgrades older format versions. Newer saves are refused with a clear message.
- *Format 2 (Milestone 5):* readers take each section's version: `IDS` 2 adds enemy ids, `COLN` 2 egg castes, the soldier share and threat statistics, `ANTS` 2 caste and health, `BLDG` 2 gate health and trap timers; new `FLUD` (fluid level and kind planes) and `THRT` (raid timer and state, enemies). Format 1 saves load with defaults (full health, workers, no fluid).
- *Format 3 (Milestone 6):* `COLN` 3 adds every caste's share and hatch count, cells scouted and which eggs lie in a nursery (by the nursery's origin cell, resolved after buildings load); `ANTS` 3 the carried count and a carried egg (caste, age); new `PHER` (one strength plane per pheromone kind). Emitters keep their pheromone kind in the filter slot (not remapped as an item). Castes are stored by their enum value. Possession and a running Pheromone Burst are not saved. Format 1 and 2 saves load (the soldier share becomes the soldiers' caste share).
- *Format 4 (Milestone 7):* `COLN` 4 adds the princesses and princesses raised; new `LGCY` holds the legacy (memory, flights, records, trait levels by id) and the colony's world modifiers by id. Their effects (stats, princess limit, Vein Instinct, biome scale) are re-applied on load; starting bonuses are not. Older saves load with an empty legacy.
- *Format 5 (Milestone 10):* `COLN` 5 adds each storage cell's filter (allowed items by id, or "anything") and the reserve per item; chests are no longer in the container list. `BLDG` 3 adds a chest's filter and contents (with its building) and a machine's recipe choice. Unloaders keep their item (or "any") in the filter slot. 10.2 adds `SURF` 1 (clock, weather and its timers, the bird, spiders still to come tonight, the leaf timer, the queen's honeydew, the surface random state). 10.3 writes `FLUD` 2: fires are kept on solid cells (kind *Fire*, level = burn time left) and smoke is a fluid kind; the pump / wheel fluid block flag in `WRLD` is cleared on load and set again by the buildings; ash is a material by id. 10.4 writes `ANTS` 4 (each ant's XP, skill, trait and lane; older ants get no skill or trait and a lane from their random state) and a new section `QUEN` 1 (evolution levels by id, the six job priorities), read right after research so its unlocks come before the caste shares. 10.5 writes `BLDG` 4 (the Ancient Engine's stage, whether a step is being built and its work total), `THRT` 2 (a thief's loot, a digger's progress, stuck time and trail, whether the raid is rival ants, items lost, the mole cricket timer) and a new section `RUIN` 1 (items studied by id, lore fragments found by id, rival raids beaten). Format 1–4 saves (and 10.1 saves without `SURF`) load (no filters, no reserves; the morning of day 1, clear weather).
- **Content is stored by id**: an id table per content kind maps saved indices to the current ones, so adding or reordering content never breaks saves; content that no longer exists is dropped (unknown materials become the first biome's base material).
- The world grid is stored as **planes** (material, density, flags), which compress well: a fresh 1024×1536 world is ~270 KB. Derived state (light, flow fields, belt network, items in transit) is rebuilt on load.
- *Format 6 (Milestone 10.6):* the header adds the bloodline's **generation** (flights + 1) so the slot picker reads it without loading; `LGCY` 2 adds the colony's respecs. Format 1–5 saves load (generation unknown).
- **Autosave** every 2 minutes and on quit; the main thread only copies the state (~1.5 ms), compression and writing run on a worker thread (~120 ms). Writes go to a temp file that replaces the save, keeping the previous one as a **backup** (`.bak`), and an older copy (`.old`) is refreshed at most every 30 minutes; loading tries main, then `.bak`, then `.old`.
- **Save slots** (10.6) ✅: three slots, each its own bloodline (`colony.sav`, `colony2.sav`, `colony3.sav` in `Saves/`, so Steam Cloud's `Saves/*` rule covers them and a pre-slot save is slot 1). *Continue* opens the slot saved last; the title screen's slot picker shows each slot's generation, ants, depth, play time and age, and plays, deletes (two clicks) or starts a new bloodline in an empty slot.
