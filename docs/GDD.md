## 1. Vision

You are the mind of an ant colony. You start with a queen and a handful of workers in a small hole under the grass. You dig down through increasingly strange layers of earth, discover ores and secrets, and turn your colony into a living machine: ants and silk conveyors carry resources through tunnels to processing chambers and to the **Queen**, who turns them into eggs, new castes, and evolutions.

The fun comes from three layers stacked on top of each other:

- ✅ **Active play** — you personally dig, click, use abilities, react to events, and fight threats. It feels juicy and responsive. *(Click-to-dig with combo and debris, four abilities, four random events, raids and cave creatures, and ant possession: take control of any ant and dig, carry and fight with it yourself.)*
- 🟡 **Automation** — you design tunnels, conveyors, lifts and production chains so the colony runs itself. Watching resources flow to the queen should be satisfying, like Factorio in an ant farm. *(Ants mine, haul and build; specialist castes; silk belts, root lifts, splitters, filters, a smelter and a fungus garden; pheromone trails and priorities kept alive by emitters. Longer chains and buffers not yet.)*
- ✅ **Incremental progression** — numbers still go up, but through *unlocks, new mechanics, new biomes and prestige*, not just "+10% mining speed". *(Research tree with unlocks and stat upgrades, contracts, offline progress, seven biomes, and the Nuptial Flight prestige with a Genetic Memory tree and world modifiers.)*

Design pillars:
1. ✅ **Always something to do** — a new goal is always visible on screen. *(The contract board.)*
2. 🟡 **Watchable** — the colony is alive; hundreds of ants climbing walls, carrying things, reacting. *(Climbing, carrying, foraging, eggs done; reactions and more behaviours to come.)*
3. ✅ **Depth = discovery** — every layer down introduces new materials, mechanics and dangers. *(Seven biomes: water, sand, gas, lava, ruins and a creature per deep layer.)*
4. 🟡 **Automate what you've mastered** — anything you did by hand early becomes automatable later. *(Belts and machines take over hauling and processing, emitters keep scents alive; genetic traits make early research inherited and let ants follow ore veins on their own (Vein Instinct). More later.)*

## 2. Game design

### 2.1 Core loop
✅ Dig → discover resources → haul them (ants, then conveyors) → process them in chambers → feed the Queen and build/research → get more ants, castes and tech → dig deeper → hit a wall → **prestige** (Nuptial Flight) → start a new colony stronger and with new options.
*(The whole loop exists: princesses raised in a Princess Chamber fly off, the Genetic Memory they earn buys permanent traits, and the next colony starts on a new world with the modifiers the player picked. See 2.12.1.)*

### 2.2 The world (side cross-section)
- ✅ Procedurally generated from a seed, wide horizontally (1024 cells), deep vertically (1536 cells), with a **surface** at the top (grass and sky).
  - Day/night and weather are not done yet (see 2.8).
- ✅ **Depth layers / biomes**, each with its own materials, hazards, enemies and visual palette (details in 2.9.1):
  1. ✅ Topsoil & roots — dirt, pebbles, grubs, copper, sand *(roots as a feature not yet)*
  2. ✅ Clay & groundwater — clay, iron, sand, water pockets that flood tunnels
  3. ✅ Stone strata — hard rock, coal, fossils, amber, centipedes *(amber's ancient bugs as research/collectibles not yet)*
  4. 🟡 Crystal caverns — big caves full of light-emitting crystals, lakes, cave spiders *(resonance mechanics not yet)*
  5. ✅ Fungal depths — glowing giant fungi (food), spore gas pockets, swarms of fungal mites (the hostile underground species)
  6. 🟡 Magma zone — basalt, obsidian, lava lakes, gas pockets, fire beetles *(heat powering smelting not yet)*
  7. 🟡 Ancient ruins — rooms of a lost ant civilization, relics (knowledge), ruin guardians *(story and endgame tech not yet)*
- 🟡 Materials have hardness, value, and special properties (light-emitting, flammable, conductive, unstable). *(Hardness, value, light emission and unstable (sand collapses) work; flammable/conductive are flags without behaviour yet.)*
- 🟡 **Cave features**: natural caverns ✅, underground lakes ✅, gas pockets ✅, lava lakes ✅, collapsing sand ✅, ruin rooms ✅; roots not yet.
- 🟡 **Fluids**: cellular water, lava and gas that flow into tunnels. Flooding is a hazard ✅, and later a resource (water wheels / irrigation for fungus farms, magma heat) — not yet.
- ✅ **Fog of war / darkness**: unexplored areas are dark; breaking into a hidden cave reveals it. Light matters (see 3.2).

### 2.3 Ants and castes
- ✅ Ants are individual agents that **climb walls and ceilings** — they crawl on any surface of a dug tunnel, including the back wall (like an ant farm), so they take direct routes through chambers. Above ground they must touch the ground.
- 🟡 Castes unlocked over time: **Worker** (dig/haul) ✅, **Miner** (fast digging) ✅, **Hauler** (carries more) ✅, **Nurse** (brood care) ✅, **Builder** (constructs chambers/conveyors) ✅, **Soldier** (defense) ✅, **Scout** (explores, reveals map, finds events) ✅, **Engineer** (maintains machines) ✅, plus late castes (e.g. glow ants that carry light, heat-resistant ants for magma) — not yet. *(See 2.6.1.)*
- 🟡 Ants have simple **needs** (food, rest) and gain **XP/levels** in their job. Optional: small randomized traits ("Strong", "Lazy", "Lucky"). *(Every ant has hit points and regenerates; food is a colony-wide upkeep per caste, not a per-ant need; no XP or traits yet.)*
- ✅ **Job system** with priorities. The player gives orders mainly through **designations** (dig here, build this) and **pheromone painting** (see 2.6), not by micromanaging individuals. *(Mining orders, storage zones, blueprints, dig/haul/build jobs with weights and openings; Dig / Gather scents put jobs first, Avoid keeps ants out; job weights per caste.)*
- ✅ The player can **possess** any ant (click to take control) for direct active play: run, dig, carry, fight. This is the main "active" mode and should feel good to control. *(See 2.6.1; it uses the same crawling rules as every ant.)*

### 2.4 The Queen
- ✅ The heart of the colony and the main resource sink. Lives in the Royal Chamber (the starting nest).
- 🟡 Consumes food and special resources → lays eggs → eggs need nurses and brood chambers to hatch. *(Food → eggs of the caste mix the player sets → ants. Nurses carry eggs to Nurseries where they hatch faster; nurses are a boost, not a requirement (decision M6). Special resources not yet.)*
- **Queen Evolution tree**: spend rare resources to unlock castes, bigger broods, passive bonuses, new abilities. *(Castes are unlocked by research for now.)*
- ✅ Visually central: conveyors and haulers feeding her, eggs being carried away. If she starves, production slows dramatically (clear feedback, not instant game over).

### 2.5 Logistics and automation
- ✅ **Early**: ants carry everything by hand. Visible bottlenecks motivate automation.
- ✅ **Silk conveyors**: belts laid along tunnels (left/right); items visibly ride them.
- ✅ **Root lifts / elevators**: vertical transport (up/down) for deep mining.
- 🟡 **Splitters, filters, mergers, buffers** for routing. *(Splitters, filters and merging (belts feeding into one) done; buffers / chests not yet.)*
- ✅ **Storage chambers** with capacity: items are kept physically in storage cells (8 each, any type, sorted by placement) and in the royal chamber, and drawn there.
- ✅ **Pheromone highways**: painted trails that make ants travel faster and prefer routes.
- 🟡 Throughput stats and overlays (show flow rates, blocked belts, idle ants) so optimizing feels like a puzzle. *(Flow overlay, income rates per item and a hover panel with per-belt flow done; numbers drawn in the world not yet.)*

### 2.5.1 Colony basics (as built in Milestone 2) ✅
- **Resources**: digging a cell may drop an item (`TerrainMaterialDef.drop`): ores, coal, amber, fossils, crystal shards, grubs. Plain dirt/clay/stone drop nothing. Drops fall straight down to the floor as piles.
- **Food**: grub nests in the topsoil (dig them → grubs) and seeds that appear on the surface over time (`ContentDatabase.surfaceForage`), so workers go outside to forage. Food items turn into Food points on delivery.
- **Delivery**: workers haul items to the royal chamber (around the queen) or to **Storage zones** the player paints on open cells. *(Changed in M3: the stock is physical. The royal chamber holds `baseItemCapacity` (40) items and every storage cell `storageItemsPerCell` (8), any mix of types. A delivered item goes into the nearest container with room, preferring one that already holds that item, and every stored item is drawn on the back wall of its cell / the royal chamber floor. Erasing a storage cell moves its items to other containers or drops them. Food stays as points with its own capacity.)* Ants only pick up what can still be delivered.
- **Orders**: the player paints **Mine** orders on solid cells (also into the fog, to plan tunnels ahead) and Storage zones; right mouse erases. Personal click-dig stays as the **Dig** tool.
- **Queen**: consumes food to lay eggs on the chamber floor; eggs hatch into workers. Workers and queen have food upkeep. At zero food the colony is **starving**: work slows and laying stops (no game over).
- **Jobs**: idle workers choose dig or haul by path distance and priority weights, limited by open slots (a few diggers per frontier cell, one hauler per item) so crowds don't queue behind a narrow tunnel front.
- **Watchability**: colony sprites never render darker than a minimum brightness, so the colony stays readable in dark tunnels while terrain darkness still shows where light is.

### 2.5.2 Logistics (as built in Milestone 3) ✅
- **Building**: the Build tool places **blueprints**. The cost is paid from the stock when placed; workers walk over and build it (a build job next to dig and haul). Removing a building refunds its cost (blueprints always in full; finished buildings by `refundFraction`, default 100%).
- **Transport cells** (1 cell, on open walkable cells, underground or on the ground surface): **Silk Belt** (left/right), **Root Lift** (up/down), **Splitter**, **Filter**. A cell holds up to 2 items. Items move along the cell and pass to the next transport cell; when the next one is full they wait, so jams back up the line. Belts feeding into one cell merge. Two belts pointing at each other never connect.
- **End of a line**: a belt or lift whose next cell has no transport delivers into a **machine** there, or into the **stock** if the line ends in (or next to) the royal chamber or a storage zone. Otherwise the line leads nowhere and items stop at its end.
- **Splitter**: hands items in turn to every neighbouring belt, lift or machine that is not feeding into it. **Filter**: items of its chosen type leave only in its arrow direction; everything else goes to its other sides. Splitters and filters do not connect directly to each other.
- **Routing masks**: every transport cell knows which items can reach a sink from it. A splitter only sends an item where it can be delivered; workers only load belts that can deliver what they carry; falling drops only land on such belts.
- **Workers and belts**: a carrying worker picks once between the stock (royal chamber, storage zones and belts leading there) and a machine that needs the item (or a belt feeding one), preferring machines unless they are much farther away (`consumerBias`). Workers drop items onto the nearest suitable belt instead of walking all the way. Drops from digging fall onto a belt or lift they pass. A worker with nowhere to deliver puts its load down as a pile.
- **Machines** (multi-cell, on a flat floor): input buffer per item (two crafts' worth); they pick the first craftable recipe in turn. Products go onto a transport cell next to the machine that leads away from it, or are left as a pile on the machine floor for workers (production pauses at `outputLimit`). Machines are fed by belts, by workers bringing fresh piles, and by workers **fetching from storage**: a supply job (per consumed item, a flow field to the containers holding it) takes an item out of the container the worker reaches and carries it to the machine, as long as machines have room for more than is already on its way.
- **Content**: Smelter (3×3): 2 copper ore → 1 copper; 2 iron ore + 1 coal → 1 iron. Fungus Garden (4×2): 2 seeds → 3 fungus (3 food each), the first food multiplier. Copper is used for filters; iron has no use yet.
- **Overlay (O)**: belts coloured from grey (idle) to green (flowing), red when jammed; machines green (working), yellow (waiting for input), red (output blocked); idle workers get a blue marker. The resource bar shows income per minute (net for food).

### 2.6 Pheromones (signature mechanic) ✅
The player paints pheromone fields onto the map: *Trail*, *Dig*, *Gather*, *Defend*, *Avoid*. Ants follow gradients. This is the main way to direct the colony and should be thematic and fun. Pheromones fade over time unless sustained by a **Pheromone Emitter** building (automation of orders).
*(Changed in M6: **Trail** (the highways of 2.5) replaces *Haul to*, which storage zones already do. Scents do not diffuse: painted areas stay sharp and predictable, and the flow fields read them as on / off. Details in 2.6.1.)*

### 2.6.1 Pheromones & castes (as built in Milestone 6) ✅
- **Pheromones** (`PheromoneSystem`): one strength byte per kind and cell (255 = fresh). The Pheromone tool (5) paints a round brush on cells the player can see; right mouse erases that kind. Strength drops by `decayPerSecond` (1) once a second, so a painted scent lasts about 4 minutes; a Burst job only visits chunks that hold some. Ants carrying something refresh the trail cell they step on (+6). A **Pheromone Emitter** (research *Scent Glands*) keeps every cell of its kind within 10 cells at full strength (paint first, then the emitter keeps it) and always scents its own spot; R picks the kind of new ones, clicking a placed one changes it.
  - **Trail**: a step between two trail cells costs half in the flow fields (1 / 2 instead of 2 / 3) and ants walk 50% faster on it.
  - **Dig** / **Gather**: mining orders / item piles inside the scent are sources at distance 0; all other ones start "late" at 40 cells (`priorityCells`), so scented jobs win unless a plain one is 40 cells closer. Arrival is detected at local minima, so late sources still work.
  - **Defend**: scented cells join the guard field; idle soldiers patrol there (like guard posts). Only available once soldiers are researched.
  - **Avoid**: a step touching an Avoid cell costs 4×; no dig, haul or build job is taken inside; wandering ants never step in. Fields enemies follow (home, exit, ants, enemies) ignore all pheromones, so scents can't steer raiders.
  - Drawn by the terrain shader as static speckles in the scent's colour (faint normally, clearer with the Pheromone tool), mirrored to the GPU as 2 bits per kind and cell.
- **Castes** are data (`CasteDef`): egg cost, upkeep, hit points, bite, speed / dig / build multipliers, items carried, job weights (0 = never) and specialist numbers. Workers and soldiers took over the numbers that were in `GameConfig`. Specialists are unlocked by research. The player sets a **share** per caste in the castes panel (K, 5% steps, at most 50% each and 80% together; new castes start at 10%, soldiers 20%); the queen lays whichever unlocked caste is furthest below its share (by at least half an ant), else a worker.
  | Caste | Egg | Upkeep /min | HP | Specials |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Worker | 6 | 0.25 | 40 | digs, hauls, builds, supplies |
  | Soldier | 15 | 0.6 | 150 | bite 14; fights, guards; no jobs |
  | Miner | 9 | 0.35 | 50 | digs 3× as fast; only digs |
  | Hauler | 8 | 0.3 | 45 | carries 3 of an item, 20% faster; hauls and supplies only |
  | Nurse | 6 | 0.2 | 30 | carries eggs to a Nursery |
  | Builder | 8 | 0.3 | 45 | builds 3× as fast; only builds |
  | Scout | 8 | 0.3 | 35 | 50% faster; senses rock within 10 cells; catches golden beetles |
  | Engineer | 10 | 0.35 | 40 | tends a machine: +50% speed while there (one per machine) |
- **Nurses & Nurseries**: a Nursery (3×2, on a flat floor) holds 6 eggs (two per silk cradle). Nurses carry eggs from the royal chamber there; eggs in a nursery hatch 50% faster and no longer count toward the queen's egg limit, so she keeps laying. Eggs age while carried; a nurse that dies drops its egg.
- **Scouts** walk to the edge of the known tunnels (a "frontier" field: explored tunnel cells with hidden rock 8 cells away, recomputed by a parallel Burst job at most every 3 s) and twice a second reveal every hidden cell within 10 cells; ore found that way glows like Seismic Sense. They chase and catch golden beetles.
- **Engineers** walk to a machine without one and stand by it; the machine works 50% faster while tended (`engineersPerMachine` = 1).
- **Haulers** take up to three of the same item from a pile (or from storage for machines, as many as machines still want) and hand them over one at a time; what is left looks for the next destination.
- **Possession** (F on an ant, or the toolbar button and a click): the ant takes no AI decisions; the pan keys push it to the neighbour cell that best matches the direction, with the same crawling rules as every ant (walls, ceilings, falling). Left mouse bites toward the cursor within 2.5 cells: an enemy there takes 6 damage, else the rock is dug like the Dig tool (power 0.9, the combo builds, 6 bites per second). Right mouse or Space picks up a pile at or next to the ant (anything, even if the stock is full), or delivers / puts down what it carries. It walks 60% faster and takes half damage. The camera follows it, a warm Light2D (no shadows) goes with it and a blinking gold marker floats over it. F / Esc lets go; it is released if it dies and is not kept in saves.
- **Pheromone Burst** (V, research): every ant within 20 cells of the cursor drops what it does and rushes to the spot (50% faster) for 15 s, then picks a job there. 150 s cooldown. No ants in range: no effect, no cooldown.
- **Research** (8 nodes in rows 4–6 of tiers 2–4): Miner, Hauler, Builder, Nurse (+ Nursery), Scout and Engineer Caste, Scent Glands (Emitter), Pheromone Burst. **Contracts**: hatch miners / haulers / nurses (new kind *hatch caste*, gated by the caste's research).
- **Offline**: castes keep their shares; eggs carried by nurses hatch too; pheromones do not fade while the game is closed.

### 2.7 Chambers and buildings
🟡 Dig out a space, designate it as a chamber type. Chamber size and decoration affect efficiency. *(Royal chamber (fixed) and Storage zones done; machines are placed as buildings on a dug-out floor; size/decoration bonuses not yet.)*
Examples: Royal Chamber ✅, Brood Nursery ✅ *(the Nursery building)*, Storage ✅, Fungus Farm (food) ✅ *(as the Fungus Garden, fed with seeds instead of leaves)*, Aphid Ranch (honeydew), Crusher, Smelter ✅ *(coal is an ingredient; heat from magma not yet)*, Crystal Resonator, Research Lab ✅ *(the Archive: studies finds into knowledge)*, Barracks, Fossil Museum (collection bonuses), Pheromone Emitter ✅.
🟡 Production chains should grow from simple (dirt → nothing; copper ore → copper) to multi-step (iron ore + coal heat → iron → reinforced conveyors). *(Copper ore → copper → knowledge, iron ore + coal → iron done; iron and coal are spent on research; reinforced conveyors not yet.)*

### 2.8 Surface
- Day/night cycle and weather (rain floods upper tunnels, drought reduces food).
- 🟡 **Foraging expeditions** to the surface for food ✅ (seeds), leaves (fungus feed) and other forage not yet.
- 🟡 Surface threats: birds, spiders, rival ant colonies. *(Raids by a rival termite mound done; birds and surface spiders not yet.)*

### 2.9 Threats and combat
- ✅ Raids from rival colonies, termites, centipedes, beetles; deep layers have their own creatures.
- ✅ Defense: soldier ants, gates, traps, chokepoint design. Threats escalate with depth and colony size.
- 🟡 Hazards: cave-ins in unstable materials ✅, flooding ✅, gas ✅, lava ✅; heat not yet.
- ✅ Losing ants is a setback, not a disaster. The tone is cozy-but-challenging.

### 2.9.1 Depth & danger (as built in Milestone 5) ✅
- **Biomes** (depth below the surface, borders warped by noise): topsoil 0–110, clay 110–290, stone strata 290–690, **crystal caverns** 690–870 (quartzite, big caves, dense glow crystals, water), **fungal depths** 870–1050 (soft mycelium, glowing giant fungus that drops 2 fungus, spore gas, water), **magma zone** 1050–1250 (basalt, obsidian, glowing magma rock, lava lakes, gas), **ancient ruins** to the bottom (ruin brick in a grid of rooms with doors and hatches instead of noise caves, relics, glow crystals). **Sand** (unstable, soft) appears in the topsoil and clay. Biome extras are data on `BiomeDef`: fluid pockets, a ruin-room grid, and creature lists.
- **Fluids** (`FluidSystem`): water, lava and gas, one kind and a level 0–255 per open cell. A Burst cellular automaton runs 10 times a second over awake chunks only, bottom to top with alternating sweep direction. Water falls, then spreads sideways (half / thirds of the difference); lava does the same every 4th step, only above a difference of 24 and at half the rate, so it heaps up and creeps; gas rises and spreads under ceilings. Water films of 8/255 or less evaporate and gas thins out, but only in explored tunnels (untouched pockets stay). Where lava meets water the lava cell turns into **obsidian** and the water loses 96/255 as steam. Chunks sleep 12 steps after their last change and wake when terrain near them opens; chunks the player has not revealed at all sleep while playing (hidden lakes finish settling when the colony gets close). World generation fills cave regions by noise per biome, drops liquids to the floor / gas to the ceiling per column, then runs up to 400 steps so lakes start settled. *(Decision: a separate 2-byte fluid plane next to the 4-byte cells (both mirrored to the GPU) rather than widening the cell; no pressure, so water does not rise in U-bends.)*
- **Hazards**: water or gas at 50% or more and any lava **block ant paths** (flow fields avoid them; field rebuilds for moving fluid are throttled to once a second). An ant caught inside swims (55% speed) to the dry neighbour nearest home, else up (down out of gas). Damage per second: drowning 6 (deep water), lava 40, gas 4 (from 19% density). **Cave-ins**: when a cell is dug out, unstable cells above it fall one cell per fluid step, sliding diagonally off piles; they never fall into buildings, blueprints, storage or the royal chamber; whoever they land on takes 25 damage and is pushed up; fallen sand is ordered for mining automatically.
- **Health**: workers 40 HP, soldiers 150 HP; ants regenerate 2 HP/s after 6 s without damage. Dead ants drop what they carried.
- **Enemies** (`EnemyDef`, `EnemySystem`): **cave creatures** appear when the colony breaks into a cave (one per N cave cells of that biome, capped per cave, at least 8 cells from the breach), wait dormant until an ant comes within their aggro radius, then hunt along an "ants" flow field and wander near home when they lose their prey. **Raids** (`ThreatConfig`): after 12 minutes and with at least 20 ants, every 7–10 minutes a rival termite raid arrives on the surface 60–100 cells from the nest after a 30 s warning; size 3 + 0.08 per ant + 2 per biome reached below the first, at most 40. Raiders walk to the royal chamber, eat 1 food/s each (the queen stops laying meanwhile) and leave along an exit field once they have eaten 8 or the food is gone. A raid where every raider dies before eating is **repelled** (+10 knowledge). Enemies bite the ant next to them; every ant next to an enemy bites back (workers 3, soldiers 14 damage per second). Killed enemies drop chitin (deep ones also obsidian or relics) and knowledge.
  | Enemy | Where | HP | Bite | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Termite Raider | raids | 22 | 5 / 1 s | eats food, leaves when full |
  | Centipede | stone strata | 60 | 8 / 1 s | |
  | Cave Spider | crystal caverns | 55 | 10 / 0.8 s | fast, aggro 16 |
  | Fungal Mite | fungal depths | 14 | 3 / 0.7 s | swarms (up to 8 per cave), gas-proof |
  | Fire Beetle | magma zone | 160 | 15 / 1.2 s | walks through lava |
  | Ruin Guardian | ancient ruins | 320 | 24 / 1.5 s | drops a relic |
- **Soldiers**: research *Soldier Caste* unlocks them; the player sets the **soldier share** (0–50% in 10% steps, default 20%) and the queen lays a soldier egg (15 food) whenever soldiers (hatched and in eggs) are below that share. Soldiers eat 0.6 food/min, never take jobs, go after any enemy within 60 cells (path distance), bite when next to one, and otherwise wait around the nearest **Guard Post** (or the queen). *(Changed in M6: the soldier share is one of the caste shares (5% steps, castes panel) and its numbers live in the soldier `CasteDef`; soldiers also patrol Defend scent.)*
- **Defense buildings** (single cells, `BuildingKind` Gate / Trap / GuardPost / Wall): **Earth Wall** (free, from the start) is built like a blueprint and then turns its cell into solid packed earth (seal floods, gas, caves; mine it to reopen). **Resin Gate**: ants pass, enemies must break it (150 HP, mends 1 HP/s); a broken gate turns back into a blueprint that workers rebuild for free. **Resin Trap** (on a floor): the first enemy stepping on it takes 30 damage and is held 4 s; re-arms after 8 s. **Guard Post**: rally point for idle soldiers.
- **Research tiers 5–6**: Soldier Caste, Fortification (gate, trap), Air Sacs (half hazard damage), Hardened Carapace (+50% ant HP), Venom Glands (+50% bite), Obsidian Plating (gates ×2 HP, traps ×2 damage). New stats `AntHealth`, `AntAttack`, `HazardResistance`, `DefenseStrength`; new research effect *unlock caste*. Contracts: defeat enemies (new kind), deliver chitin, crystal shards, obsidian, relics. The Archive studies a relic for 25 knowledge.
- **Offline**: no raids, creatures or hazards happen while the game is closed; eggs keep their caste and new eggs follow the soldier share.

### 2.10 Active incremental layer
- ✅ **Click/hold to dig** with combo multipliers and satisfying feedback (debris, screen shake).
- ✅ **Active abilities** with cooldowns (e.g. *Frenzy* — all ants work faster; *Pheromone Burst*; *Earthquake* — reveals ore in radius; *Royal Decree* — queen lays a burst of eggs). *(All four; Seismic Sense is the "Earthquake", Pheromone Burst calls nearby ants to a spot.)*
- 🟡 **Random events** that reward attention: rich ore vein exposed, trapped ant rescue, a wandering beetle carrying loot, meteorite strike on the surface, golden aphid. *(The first four done; the golden aphid needs aphids.)*
- ✅ **Contracts / goals**: a rotating list of objectives ("Deliver 500 iron to the Queen", "Reach depth 200") with rewards. There must always be a next goal.
- ✅ **Offline progress** (calculated on load, with a summary screen), since this is an incremental game on Steam.

### 2.10.1 Incremental layer (as built in Milestone 4) ✅
- **Knowledge** is a colony resource (like food, not an item). It comes from the **Archive** (a machine whose recipes output knowledge instead of items: copper 1, crystal shard 4, amber 5, fossil 6, star fragment 12), from contract rewards and from the golden beetle. *(Changed: no separate "knowledge from ruins" yet; the Archive is a placed building, not a chamber type.)*
- **Research tree v1** (16 nodes in 4 tiers, `ResearchDef`): paid once with knowledge plus items / food from the stock, instant. Effects are data: **unlock a building** (Fungus Garden, Splitter, Filter now start locked), **unlock an ability**, or **add to a colony stat** (`StatKind`: worker speed / digging, player digging, build speed, belt speed, machine speed, royal capacity, food capacity, laying speed, max eggs, forage rate, offline efficiency / hours, contract slots, knowledge gain, ability recharge). Systems read the totals from one `Modifiers` table. Iron and coal get their first use here.
- **Contracts** (`ContractDef`): kinds deliver item, make item, reach depth, dig deeper, hatch ants, build a building, dig cells; measured against lifetime colony stats from the moment the contract appears. A short **intro chain** (one at a time, in order) teaches the basics; the other slots draw **repeatable** contracts at random by weight, gated by depth / research / unlocked buildings, each repeat ~30% bigger with bigger rewards. Finished contracts wait for **Claim** (rewards: knowledge, food, items).
- **Abilities** (`AbilityDef`, hotkeys Z / X / C (V for the fourth, M6), cast at the cursor): **Frenzy** (+75% worker move / dig / build for 20 s, 120 s cooldown), **Seismic Sense** (reveals ore, grub nests and crystals in a 24-cell radius through the fog; found cells glow faintly; 90 s), **Royal Decree** (4 free eggs at once; 240 s). Abilities can cost food.
- **Random events** (`RandomEventDef`): every 3–6 minutes (first after 2.5), weighted, gated by depth, at most 2 waiting. **Meteorite** (crater and a lump of meteorite rock near the nest; it drops star fragments), **Rich vein** (an ore blob by depth layer next to the tunnels, revealed), **Golden beetle** (a critter wandering the tunnels for 45 s; click it for knowledge, food and amber), **Trapped ants** (5 ants in a sealed pocket near the tunnels; connect it to the nest within 150 s or they are lost). Event cards on the HUD with a timer and a Go button.

Storage limits what arrives; the queen keeps laying while 10 minutes of upkeep stay in store; waiting eggs hatch; ability cooldowns and event timers run. A "Welcome back" panel summarises it. *(Decision: a rate replay instead of a coarse simulation keeps it cheap and predictable; it ignores that mining orders may run out or machines may run dry.)*
- **Esc menu** pauses the game (resume, save, load, new colony, save and quit).

### 2.12.1 Prestige (as built in Milestone 7) ✅
- **Princesses**: research **Nuptial Flight** (tier 6: 80 knowledge, 6 iron; needs Fungiculture and Royal Jelly) unlocks the **Princess Chamber** (4×3, 10 copper + 4 iron). Its recipe *Raise a princess* (12 fungus + 2 amber, 60 s) outputs a princess instead of items (`RecipeDef.princesses`). Princesses are a colony counter (not ants); the chamber pauses while princesses (plus those being raised) reach the limit, `maxPrincesses` (5) + traits. Each chamber shows up to 3 resting princesses.
- **Genetic Memory from a flight** (`PrestigeConfig`): score = deepest dig × 0.1 + knowledge earned × 0.1 + research done × 2 + contracts × 1; memory = floor(√(score / 4) × princess factor × gain), where the princess factor is 1 + 0.25 per princess after the first (counted up to the limit; 0 without princesses) and gain is the `GeneticGain` stat (traits, modifiers). A flight needs at least 1 princess and at least 1 memory. Starting knowledge from traits is not "earned".
- **The flight** (N screen): shows the breakdown, lets the player pick world modifiers (1 slot + traits), and after a confirming second click plays a short flight (princesses fly out of the nest shaft into the sky, 4.5 s, the simulation paused), then founds the next colony on a new random seed with the chosen modifiers and saves at once. `Simulation.TryNuptialFlight` returns the next colony's legacy and leaves the flown colony untouched.
- **Legacy** (`Legacy`): unspent memory, memory earned, flights, every trait's level, best depth and best flight. It is saved inside every colony's save (section `LGCY`), so a flight is atomic: until the new colony is saved, the old save still holds the old colony and the old legacy. *New colony* from the menu keeps the legacy (no memory for the abandoned colony, no modifiers).
- **Genetic traits** (`GeneticDef`, 19 in 4 tiers, levels with growing cost): effects are data — colony **stats** (worker digging / speed, food capacity, laying, max eggs, belt / machine speed, offline efficiency, Genetic Memory gain), **starting** workers / food / knowledge / items and a revealed radius around the nest, **inherited research** (Fungiculture, Silk Routing, Miner and Soldier Caste: done in every colony and at once when bought), extra **modifier slots**, extra **princesses**, **unlocking** world modifiers, and the new mechanic **Vein Instinct** (ore within 2 cells of every dug-out cell is ordered for mining, so ants follow veins). Bought any time: stats and inherited research apply at once, starting bonuses from the next colony.
- **World modifiers** (`WorldModifierDef`, 9): world generation offsets (ore deposit threshold for materials that drop a non-food item, cave threshold, water / lava / gas pocket thresholds, biome thickness multiplier) and colony stats (new stats `RaidSize`, `RaidFrequency`, `CreatureCount`, `GeneticGain`, plus forage). Five start unlocked (Rich Veins, Lush Meadow, Shallow World, Hollow Earth, Quiet Surface); four harsh ones (Flooded Depths, Volcanic, Famine, Swarming) come with the trait *Strange Worlds*. Harder worlds add Genetic Memory gain. Biome lookups at runtime use the world's thickness scale (`Simulation.BiomeAtDepth`).

## 3. Technical design

### 3.0 Platform, art direction and scale ✅
- **Engine**: Unity 6.6 (6000.6.x, "Supported" stream), URP 17 with the 2D Renderer. Decision: stay on 6.6 and move to the next LTS when it ships rather than downgrading to 6.3 LTS.
- **Art direction: pixel art.** Everything (terrain, ants, items, buildings, UI accents) is drawn on a single pixel grid.
- **Scale**: 16 art pixels per world unit (PPU 16). **1 cell = 8×8 px = 0.5 world units.** An ant is 12×7 px (~1.5 cells long). World 1024 × 1536 cells.
- **Pixel-perfect camera**: zoom levels are integer screen-pixels-per-art-pixel (×1…×8); the camera position snaps to the art-pixel grid. At ×1 on 1080p the view shows ~240 × 135 cells, which is the "overview" zoom. A strategic map beyond that is a separate render, not fractional zoom.

### 3.1 No Tilemaps ✅
The original used Unity Tilemaps and they were a headache (no proper shadows, hard to make look good). **Do not use Tilemaps.** Instead:

- ✅ **World data**: a chunked grid (32×32 cells per chunk, chunk-major so a chunk is one contiguous range) of 4-byte cells: material, density (dig progress), flags (revealed, explored, mining order, storage, blueprint, scanned). This is the single source of truth. *(Fluid lives in a parallel 2-byte plane (level, kind) with the same indexing, uploaded per dirty chunk like the cells.)*
  - A cell is either solid (density eroding from 255 down to 128) or fully dug (0). There is no half-open state.
- ✅ **Terrain rendering: GPU per-pixel "pixel marching squares".** *(Changed from per-chunk procedural meshes because of the pixel-art direction: smooth mesh contours would cut through art pixels.)*
  - Cell data and the light map are mirrored into GPU structured buffers. Only dirty chunks are uploaded, one 1024-cell range each, so digging never re-uploads the whole world.
  - A terrain shader on one world-sized quad snaps every fragment to the art-pixel grid, interpolates the densities of the surrounding cells (the marching-squares field) and thresholds it with smooth value noise. The result is organic, rounded tunnel edges that are still crisp pixel art.
  - Material boundaries are sampled at a smoothly jittered point, so pockets of another material read as blobs, not squares.
  - Surface pixels come from per-material **pixel textures in a texture array**, tiled in world pixel space. Edge pixels get outline/rim colors. **Normals are derived from the density gradient** (a bevel) so `Light2D` shades the terrain without hand-drawn normal maps.
  - A darker, low-contrast **background wall** is drawn behind dug areas so tunnels have depth. Its edge against the sky uses the same rounded contour, kept just inside the terrain edge.
  - No per-chunk meshes or GameObjects for terrain.
- ✅ **Colliders: none for terrain.** *(Changed: the possessed ant will use the same grid-based crawling controller as AI ants, so control is consistent and no physics colliders are needed. Revisit only if a feature truly needs Box2D, e.g. physics debris.)*

### 3.2 Lighting and shadows
- ✅ URP **2D Renderer** with `Light2D` (a global light provides the minimum ambient).
- ✅ **Primary darkness comes from a grid light map**: light propagates from the surface (sky light) and from light sources (crystals, giant fungi, magma rock and lava now; glow ants and torches later) using a Burst flood fill (8-way, RGB), sampled by the terrain and sprite shaders. Light is quantized into a few bands per pixel to match the pixel-art look. Updates are exact and local around changes.
- **ShadowCaster2D only near the camera.** *(Changed from per-chunk everywhere: underground, solid terrain is already dark via the light map, so thousands of casters would cost a lot for almost no visible gain.)* Shadow shapes are generated in code from the cell grid only for chunks that are both in view and inside the radius of a dynamic `Light2D`, and are pooled. *(Still deferred: the only dynamic light (the possessed ant's) is small and soft enough that light passing into the rock reads as a glow. Add casters with glow ants.)*
- 🟡 Dynamic `Light2D`s add local light (possessed ant, glow ants, abilities, explosions) on top of the light map. *(The possessed ant's light done.)*
- ✅ Fog of war: unrevealed cells rendered black (a "revealed" bit in the cell flags).
- ✅ Performance budget: lighting must hold 60 FPS with a large, partially dug world *(full recompute once at start, local updates ~1.4 ms)*.

### 3.3 Simulation
- ✅ **Fixed-tick simulation** (20 ticks/sec) fully separated from rendering, which interpolates. This also makes offline progress and saving sane.
- ✅ Ants: target **several hundred to a few thousand** active ants. Store ant state in struct arrays (data-oriented), update with Burst jobs where hot. *(Rendering as built: one dynamic mesh for all colony sprites, written each frame by a Burst job, one draw call. Same cost class as instancing, but it sorts and receives 2D lights like any renderer.)* Sprites snap to the art-pixel grid and rotate in 90° steps to follow the surface. Do not use one GameObject per ant. (The possessed ant can be a normal GameObject.) *(Measured: 1,050 ants ≈ 0.1–0.15 ms per tick.)*
- ✅ **Ant update**: a parallel Burst job moves ants and decides, emitting *intents* for anything touching shared state (dig damage, pickup, delivery); a serial pass applies intents in ant order, so results are deterministic.
- ✅ **Navigation**: ants crawl through dug cells (floor, walls, ceiling and back wall) and, above ground, along the ground. **Flow fields** (distance maps with octile costs: straight 2, diagonal 3; trails 1 / 2, Avoid 4×) per destination kind: mining front, haulable items, drop-off (royal chamber + storage + belts leading there), home, construction sites, guard (posts + Defend scent), brood (eggs for nurses), nurseries with room, frontier (scouts), machines wanting an engineer, golden beetles, the Pheromone Burst spot, plus per item that a machine uses a "consumer" field (machines needing it, belts feeding them) and a "supply" field (storage holding it). All fields share one array so the Burst ant job can read any of them. Rebuilt in parallel jobs (Dial's algorithm, a ring of 16 buckets) at most every few ticks when something changed. A* for one-off paths is not needed yet.
- ✅ **Pheromones**: grid fields per pheromone type with decay, updated in jobs. *(Changed: one strength byte per kind and cell, no diffusion; flow fields read them as step costs and as "late" sources, see 2.6.1.)*
- ✅ **Fluids**: simple cellular automaton, updated at a lower rate (10 Hz), only in awake (and revealed) chunks; one single-threaded Burst job so results are deterministic. *(See 2.9.1.)*
- ✅ **Enemies**: a plain C# list stepped serially (tens to a few hundred); they read the shared flow fields (home for raiders, an "ants" field while any creature hunts, an exit field while raiders leave), and a per-tick cell → ant hash finds who is next to whom. Soldiers read an "enemies" field (rebuilt while enemies move) and a "guard" field. *(Measured: 1,000 ants 0.09 ms per tick; with 30 cave spiders, 60 soldiers and a 40-termite raid 0.24 ms.)*
- ✅ **Items on conveyors**: simulated as data (up to two item slots per transport cell, not physics objects), stepped downstream-first so lines move without gaps, and rendered by a Burst job into one mesh with the buildings. *(The belt step is plain C# for now: ~0.55 ms per tick for 6,000 loaded belt cells in the editor.)*

Build a simple pattern for IDs and lookups. *(Pattern done: stable string ids → dense runtime indices in `ContentDB`. ### 3.5 Saving ✅
Versioned save format (binary or compressed JSON) including the full world grid, ants, buildings, conveyors, research and prestige. Autosave. Save migration support from day one. Offline progress computed from the save timestamp using a simplified rate-based simulation (not full ant sim).
- *As built:* binary. An uncompressed **header** (magic `ANT2`, format version, save time UTC, seed, play time, ant count, depth: readable without loading) followed by a **deflate-compressed body of tagged sections** (`IDS`, `SIM`, `COLN`, `ANTS`, `BLDG`, `PROG`, `WRLD`, `FLUD`, `THRT`), each with its own version byte. Unknown sections are skipped and missing ones keep defaults; `SaveGame.Migrate` upgrades older format versions. Newer saves are refused with a clear message.
- *Format 2 (Milestone 5):* readers take each section's version: `IDS` 2 adds enemy ids, `COLN` 2 egg castes, the soldier share and threat statistics, `ANTS` 2 caste and health, `BLDG` 2 gate health and trap timers; new `FLUD` (fluid level and kind planes) and `THRT` (raid timer and state, enemies). Format 1 saves load with defaults (full health, workers, no fluid).
- *Format 3 (Milestone 6):* `COLN` 3 adds every caste's share and hatch count, cells scouted and which eggs lie in a nursery (by the nursery's origin cell, resolved after buildings load); `ANTS` 3 the carried count and a carried egg (caste, age); new `PHER` (one strength plane per pheromone kind). Emitters keep their pheromone kind in the filter slot (not remapped as an item). Castes are stored by their enum value. Possession and a running Pheromone Burst are not saved. Format 1 and 2 saves load (the soldier share becomes the soldiers' caste share).
- *Format 4 (Milestone 7):* `COLN` 4 adds the princesses and princesses raised; new `LGCY` holds the legacy (memory, flights, records, trait levels by id) and the colony's world modifiers by id. Their effects (stats, princess limit, Vein Instinct, biome scale) are re-applied on load; starting bonuses are not. Older saves load with an empty legacy.
- **Content is stored by id**: an id table per content kind maps saved indices to the current ones, so adding or reordering content never breaks saves; content that no longer exists is dropped (unknown materials become the first biome's base material).
- The world grid is stored as **planes** (material, density, flags), which compress well: a fresh 1024×1536 world is ~270 KB. Derived state (light, flow fields, belt network, items in transit) is rebuilt on load.
- **Autosave** every 2 minutes and on quit; the main thread only copies the state (~1.5 ms), compression and writing run on a worker thread (~120 ms). Writes go to a temp file that replaces the save, keeping the previous one as a **backup** that loads if the main file is damaged. One slot for now.
